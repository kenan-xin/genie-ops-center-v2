# Email Templates Specification

## Overview
Status of the gallery: a design artifact and a development preview (the React Email preview tool), not a screen in the product. No route, no "Send test to me" procedure, and no rate limit ship; decided 2026-09-18, reopened if an administrator asks to check their own emails. The templates themselves and their branding rules below are the deliverable of roadmap Section 3 item 6.

The branded emails Genie Ops Center sends through React Email, shown in a standalone gallery with a mail-client frame so the design can be reviewed as a recipient sees it. Six templates are Genie's: invitation (brokered and local-account variants), role granted, role removed (with a "nothing left" variant), new device sign-in, and a generic module notification. Three credential emails of local-account deployments (set password, reset password, verify email) are sent by Keycloak's built-in templates (DEC-40); they are rendered here unstyled, labeled as Keycloak's, so the reviewer sees what the recipient gets and why it looks different from the Genie emails. Genie emails read the deployment's branding: company name, product name, logo mark, primary color and its stored foreground, font, sender name, reply-to, footer text, support contact, and terms and privacy links. The sender address is the deployment's `MAIL_FROM` (`no-reply@genie.example`), the same for every tenant; the tenant sets the display name and reply-to only. The deployment's public host (`publicHost`, the host part of `PUBLIC_URL`) is used for links in the body, never for the address. Under ADR 0007 one deployment serves one customer; the gallery's two "tenants" are two sample deployments shown side by side to prove that templates follow branding.

## User Flows
- Reviewer opens the gallery and sees the template list on the left, grouped under Genie sends and Keycloak sends, with the first template selected and rendered on the right inside a mail-client frame (sender name and address, reply-to, recipient, subject, date).
- Reviewer selects a template. The frame updates with that template's subject, preheader, and body, using sample values (recipient, inviter, role, module) shown as a small legend under the frame.
- Reviewer switches the sample deployment between two sample brandings to prove that every color, name, link, and footer follows branding with no template change.
- Reviewer toggles the frame width between Phone (360px) and Desktop (600px). The email is a single column at both widths; the button is full width on phones.
- Reviewer toggles HTML and Plain text. Plain text shows the text/plain alternative Genie sends with the same content in reading order, links written out in full.
- Reviewer presses Send test to me. A toast with a manual close confirms that the test email is queued to the reviewer's address; the sample values stay in the body and the frame subject is shown with the [Test] prefix until another template or tenant is picked.
- Reviewer selects a Keycloak template. Send test to me and the Plain text toggle are disabled with the note "Sent by Keycloak's built-in template. Test it from the realm." When the selected sample deployment is brokered, the Keycloak group is marked "local-account deployments only" and says its people never receive these emails.

## UI Requirements
- Gallery page: white page, left panel with the two template groups as a vertical list (name, one-line trigger), right area with a toolbar (a select labeled Sample deployment, Phone and Desktop toggle, HTML and Plain text toggle, Send test to me) and the mail-client frame below. On phones the list becomes a select above the frame and the toolbar wraps.
- Mail-client frame: rounded card with a header block in the client's neutral style: subject as the title, a "Sent by Keycloak built-in template" badge for Keycloak emails, then From (the sender name at the deployment address `no-reply@genie.example`; for a Keycloak email the sender name and reply-to are the realm SMTP settings, and the row says so), Reply-To, To (a person of the selected tenant), Date, and the preheader shown as the preview line. Below it, the email body on a light gray canvas at the chosen width, centered.
- Genie email layout (shared by the six Genie templates): a top bar in the tenant's primary color with the logo mark and company name in the computed foreground; a white content card with a heading, one to three short paragraphs, an optional details table (label and value rows), one primary button in the primary color with the computed foreground, an expiry or fallback line under the button with the full link in mono for clients that block buttons, and a closing line; a footer in small gray text with the footer text, a support line (email or link), Terms and Privacy links, and the product name.
- Genie templates: New-device sign-in (subject "New sign-in to {productName} from a new device", trigger `core:session:new`) names the device as browser and OS ("Chrome 130 on macOS"), the IP address, and the time in the body and in Device, IP address, and Time rows, never a city; says "If this was you, no action is needed. If not, revoke the session from your account page and tell your administrator."; and has a Review sessions button to `https://{publicHost}/account#sessions`. Invitation (brokered) explains sign-in with the company account and has an Open Genie button. Invitation (local account) explains that a separate set-password email follows from the realm and has an Open Genie button. Role granted names the role, scope, and who granted it, with an Open Genie button. Role removed names the role and scope and states what the person can still open; its "nothing left" variant, listed under it and selected with a small switch, says the person has no solutions left and carries no button. Module notification carries the module name as a small label, a title, a body, and a button whose label the module supplies; the sample is the platform placeholder module `contracts` (record CTR-2024-118, link `/m/contracts/ctr-2024-118`).
- Keycloak templates: Set password, Reset password, Verify email are Keycloak's built-in templates (DEC-40) and render as the realm sends them: plain white, system font, no top bar, no logo mark, no colored button; a text heading with the realm display name (the company name, set by provisioning), the built-in wording, a plain text link, and the built-in expiry line (12 hours for set password, 30 minutes for reset, 24 hours for verify; the realm's action-token lifetimes). Nothing from branding reaches them except that the sender name and reply-to also apply to the realm SMTP settings. Each carries a Keycloak badge in the gallery list and the frame header, not in the email itself. Their links are `{keycloakUrl}/realms/{realm}/login-actions/...`, one Keycloak base for the deployment and the deployment's own realm. A branded credential email is a later option: realm localization message overrides or a custom `EmailTemplateProvider` (ADR 0006).
- Plain text view: mono text block with the heading, paragraphs, details as label: value lines, the button label with the URL on the next line, the expiry line, and the footer lines, wrapped at 72 characters.
- Contrast: the primary color and its stored foreground (`primary_foreground`, written with the color; never recomputed here) must pass AA on the button and the top bar; the gallery shows a small contrast pill per sample deployment next to the Sample deployment select.
- Fonts: a Genie email uses the deployment's font key from the approved list when the client loads web fonts, with a system sans-serif fallback stack; a Keycloak email uses the system stack only. Designed mobile first: the phone width is the base and the desktop width only widens the column. Emails always render light; dark-mode client rendering is out of scope for this round.
- Out of scope: editing templates from the admin portal, localization beyond English, attachments, digest emails, unsubscribe management (transactional only), and a dark-mode client preview.
